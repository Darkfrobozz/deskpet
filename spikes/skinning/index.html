Skinning: how flesh follows a skeleton

Three bones, one closed outline. Drag the bend slider. The outline's points are bound to bones by weights: each point is moved by a blend of the two nearest bones' transforms, so points near a joint end up halfway between what each bone wants. That blend is why flesh bends smoothly instead of creasing at the joint.

1 · rigid plates only — each bone draws its own shape. Bend it and the outer edge opens into a wedge.
2 · skinned flesh only — one continuous outline, stretched by blended bone transforms. No holes, and the outside of the bend genuinely elongates.
3 · plates over flesh — the actual design: rigid armour on top, skinned membrane underneath. Bending shows flesh through the gaps, never the desktop.
